# SWAT Plumbing — Blog Hero Image Prompts (Oct 20 – Nov 1 batch, 4 posts)

> **Same house style as the Aug–Oct batch** (see `image-prompts-blog-sep-oct.md`).
> Blog heroes are editorial object/scene photography — NOT the GBP library style:
>
> - **No people in frame.** No technicians, no hands, no homeowners.
> - **No branding.** No truck, no logo, no uniform, no shield overlay.
> - **16:9 landscape**, 1600px wide.
> - **Flat, natural light** — overcast daylight or soft interior daylight.
>   No dramatic lighting, no glow, no teal-orange grading, no lens flare.
>   The site's overlay component supplies the dark/tactical mood; the photo
>   stays clean and documentary.
> - **Real North Texas residential context** — brick and stone ranch homes,
>   Bermuda grass, live oaks, bare concrete, builder-grade interiors.
> - Subject reads clearly at a glance, with some negative space for the
>   headline overlay.
>
> **Pipeline:** generate → drop in `public/blog/` → `node scripts/convert-blog-pngs.mjs`
> (resizes to 1600w, writes .webp at q82, deletes the source).
>
> **Hero filenames are shortened, not the full post slug** — this repo's
> convention. The exact names below are what the posts reference.

---

### 1. `water-heater-capacity-fort-worth.webp` — Oct 20
Documentary photograph, 16:9. A white 50-gallon gas tank water heater standing in a residential utility closet with the door open, shot straight on from a few feet back. Flexible supply connectors and a gas line visible at the top, a temperature dial on the front of the jacket, bare concrete or vinyl floor beneath. Builder-grade beige walls, a folded towel and a laundry basket just visible at the edge of frame to place it in a lived-in house. Flat, even interior daylight spilling in from the room beyond. Negative space to the left for a headline overlay. Shot on Canon R5, 35mm, true-to-life color, fine grain. No people, no branding, no dramatic lighting.

### 2. `frozen-pipes-attic-fort-worth.webp` — Oct 24
Documentary photograph, 16:9. An uninsulated white PEX and copper water supply line running across open attic joists above blown-in fiberglass insulation in a North Texas home. Bare roof decking and rafters overhead, a gable vent at the far end letting in cold grey daylight. A short section of foam pipe sleeve has been fitted over one run and stops partway, leaving the rest bare — the detail that tells the story. Flat, cold, natural light only, no work lamp glare. Negative space along the upper left for a headline overlay. Shot on Sony A7IV, 24mm, deep focus. No people, no hands, no branding.

### 3. `cast-iron-sewer-line-fort-worth.webp` — Oct 28
Documentary photograph, 16:9. A cut section of old cast iron sewer pipe lying on bare soil beside an open excavation in a residential side yard, the cut end facing camera so the interior is visible — heavy rust-colored scale and tuberculation built up around the inside wall, the bottom of the bore corroded thin and rough. Dark damp earth, a shovel blade and loose clay spoil at the edge of frame, dormant Bermuda grass and a brick ranch wall soft in the background. Flat overcast daylight. Negative space to the right for a headline overlay. Shot on Canon R5, 50mm, sharp on the pipe interior. No people, no hands, no branding.

### 4. `backflow-preventer-freeze-aledo.webp` — Nov 1
Documentary photograph, 16:9. An above-ground brass irrigation backflow preventer assembly mounted on riser pipes beside the foundation of a brick North Texas home — two ball valve handles, a pair of small test cocks on the body, visible water staining and light oxidation on the brass. Dormant winter Bermuda grass and a bed of dark mulch at the base, bare crepe myrtle branches behind. Flat, cold, overcast daylight. Negative space to the left for a headline overlay. Shot on Canon R5, 50mm, straight-on at the assembly's height. No people, no hands, no branding.
